Purpose and Method

This report compares the Guard Tour capability built into our platform against the two commercial platforms profiled in the Guard Tour Software Research Report (Silvertrac Software and Staffr / TrackTik, both owned by Trackforce).

The two source columns are taken from that research report, which was itself compiled from vendor product documentation, help-center articles, and app-store listings. Our column is different in kind: it was derived by reading our own source code directly — the Prisma schema, the NestJS controllers and services, and the Next.js pages listed below — so it reflects what the system actually does today rather than what a feature list claims.

Our Guard Tour Architecture

Our tour model is the same fundamental loop both commercial platforms use, expressed as six related entities:

  • Checkpoint — belongs to a Site; carries name, description, location note, an optional QR code value, and an optional GPS geofence (latitude, longitude, radius in metres).
  • PatrolRoute — a named, ordered tour scoped to a Site.
  • PatrolRouteCheckpoint — the join table that carries sequenceOrder, making the tour an ordered list.
  • PatrolRun — one execution of a route by one guard on one shift, with not_started → in_progress → completed status and the guard's latest known position.
  • PatrolEvent — one checkpoint scan, recording timestamp, status, notes, and the server-computed geofence verdict.
  • PatrolEvidence — photos captured at a scan; multiple photos per checkpoint, stored on local disk with the on-disk filename never exposed to clients.

Three separate portals sit on top of this, matching the shape of both commercial products: an admin monitor, a guard field view, and a client-facing live status endpoint.

Where We Lead

Panic / duress alerting with a managed lifecycle

We have a dedicated EmergencyAlert entity with a genuine ACTIVE → ACKNOWLEDGED → RESOLVED workflow that records who acknowledged and who resolved each alert. It is deliberately independent of both incidents and patrol runs, so a guard can raise an alert with no active shift or tour. Neither vendor profile describes an equivalent; TrackTik's nearest analogue is scheduled lone-worker check-ins, which is a different and weaker safety mechanism.

Offline-first field operation

Checkpoint scans, tour completion, incident creation, and shift check-in/check-out all queue locally and replay on reconnect. Replay is idempotent — each action carries a client-generated ID, already-synced actions are skipped, and failures are recorded with an error message rather than silently dropped. This puts us clearly ahead of Silvertrac, which does not emphasise offline support at all, and on par with TrackTik.

Server-authoritative geofence verification

The guard's device submits raw coordinates only; it never submits a verdict. The server computes the outcome and writes one of five explicit states: SUCCESS, OUTSIDE_GEOFENCE, LOCATION_UNAVAILABLE, NO_GEOFENCE_CONFIGURED, or INVALID_LOCATION. Radius is configurable per checkpoint between 5 m and 2,000 m, defaulting to 50 m. This is a stronger anti-tampering posture than a client-reported pass/fail, and the distinction matters in any dispute over whether a guard was genuinely on site.

Privacy-conscious location handling

We deliberately store only the guard's latest position on the patrol run, overwritten on each update, plus one point per checkpoint scan. There is no continuous location history table. This is a defensible design choice in jurisdictions with strict worker-surveillance rules, and it is a point neither vendor makes.

Back office already integrated

Shifts, timesheets, invoices, and rate cards are part of the same platform and the same tenant model. Against Silvertrac this is a decisive advantage — Silvertrac is reporting-centric and stops at a time clock. Against TrackTik it is parity on scope, though TrackTik's modules are more mature.

Where We Fall Short

1. Physical scan verification does not existCritical

This is the single most significant gap. A qrCodeValue field exists on the Checkpoint model and is editable in the admin UI, but nothing ever reads it back. The scan endpoint accepts only notes, status, latitude, and longitude — there is no QR value in the request payload, no comparison in the service layer, and no camera scanner, barcode API, or NFC reader anywhere in the frontend. No QR or barcode library appears in the frontend dependencies.

The practical consequence is that every checkpoint we support is what Silvertrac calls a "virtual" checkpoint. A guard opens the tour, taps a button in a list, and confirms a modal. Our GPS geofence is a meaningful substitute — it does verify physical presence within a radius — but it is not the same proof as requiring contact with a tag affixed at the location, and GPS accuracy degrades badly indoors, in stairwells, and in basements, which is precisely where many checkpoints live. On a feature matrix, this is the one row where we lose outright to both competitors.

2. No proactive missed or late tour alertingCritical

Missed checkpoints are detected only reactively: when a run is completed, any unscanned checkpoint is auto-marked as missed. There is no expected-timing field on the route checkpoint model and no scheduled job anywhere in the patrols module. Both competitors alert a supervisor while the tour is running late, which is the point at which intervention is still possible. We surface the same information after the fact, on a dashboard, where it becomes a reporting statistic rather than an operational signal.

3. No per-checkpoint configurable actionsImportant

TrackTik's clearest differentiator is that each checkpoint can be configured to open a report form, pose an exception question, display a message to the officer, or fire an alert. Our Checkpoint model carries only descriptive fields — name, description, location note. Every checkpoint behaves identically. Silvertrac does not have this either, so this is a gap against the enterprise tier specifically.

4. No live mapImportant

The monitor page lists active runs and links out to Google Maps per guard. Both competitors render all guards on a single map with tour progress overlaid. Functionally this is cosmetic — the underlying location data is already there and already polling — but it is the screen that dominates every product demo, and its absence is immediately visible in a side-by-side evaluation.

5. Offline photo capture is unsupportedImportant

Checkpoint scans queue offline, but evidence upload requires connectivity. A guard patrolling a dead zone can record that they visited a checkpoint but cannot attach the photograph that proves what they found there — and by the time they regain signal, the moment has passed.

6. No native mobile applicationImportant

Our guard experience is responsive web. Both competitors ship native apps with app-store listings and public ratings. Native access matters here beyond preference: NFC reading, reliable background location, and camera integration are all substantially better supported natively, which means this gap partly gates the fixes for gaps 1 and 5.

7. Surrounding modules not in scopeMinor

We do not have parking management or a time clock with pass-down notes and post orders (Silvertrac), nor visitor management, asset tracking, or scheduled lone-worker check-ins (TrackTik). Audio evidence is also absent. None of these are core to the tour loop, and each should be treated as a separate product decision rather than a gap to close reflexively.

Summary Assessment

Our Guard Tour implements the same fundamental loop as both commercial platforms — ordered checkpoints, verified scans, real-time supervisor visibility, photo evidence, and a client-facing view. In several respects the implementation is stronger than what either vendor documents: server-authoritative geofence verification, an idempotent offline queue, a genuine panic-alert lifecycle, tenant-scoped multi-tenancy, and audit logging throughout.

Positioned against the two competitors, we sit ahead of Silvertrac on scope — we carry scheduling, timesheets, and invoicing that Silvertrac does not — and behind TrackTik on tour sophistication, lacking configurable checkpoint logic and dispatch tasking.

Two gaps, however, cut across that positioning and would be noticed by any evaluator regardless of which competitor they are comparing us to: the absence of physical scan verification, and the absence of proactive alerting while a tour is still running. Both are addressable with modest effort against the existing architecture, and both are considerably more valuable than any of the surrounding modules we currently lack.

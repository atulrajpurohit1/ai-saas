01
What AegisLead Is
Positioning

AegisLead is a single platform that runs a physical security guarding company end to end — from winning the contract, to putting guards on site and proving they were there, to getting the invoice paid.

The problem it solves

A typical guarding company runs on four or five disconnected systems: a CRM for sales, a scheduling tool for rosters, a separate guard-tour app with its own hardware, an accounting package for invoices, and a large quantity of spreadsheets and WhatsApp groups holding it all together. Nothing talks to anything else. Hours are re-keyed from a patrol app into a timesheet and again into an invoice. When a client asks "was your guard actually at the loading bay at 2am?", the answer takes someone half a day to assemble.

AegisLead replaces that stack with one system where the data only gets entered once and then flows: a lead becomes a client, a client gets sites, sites get shifts, shifts get guards, guards produce verified patrol evidence and attendance, attendance becomes an approved timesheet, and the timesheet becomes the invoice.

What makes it different

One record, four uses

A guard's check-in is simultaneously an attendance record, a shift status change, a line on the client's daily report, and the basis for a billable timesheet line. It is entered once, by the guard, on their phone.

Evidence, not assertions

Patrol checkpoints are verified two independent ways — a scanned QR code fixed to the physical checkpoint, and a GPS geofence. Both results are stored separately, so a dispute is settled with data rather than memory.

The client is inside the system

Customers get their own login to approve proposals, read approved incident reports, download daily service reports, see invoices and raise disputes — instead of being emailed PDFs.

AI where it removes real work

Not a chatbot bolted on. AI drafts the proposal, researches the prospect, transcribes and coaches the sales call, writes the daily report summary, scores the lead, evaluates subcontractor bids and recommends which guard to put on an open shift.

Who it is for

Security contractors running anywhere from a handful of guards to several hundred. The platform is multi-tenant: every company's data is completely separate, and the product is sold in three parts so a company that only wants the sales engine, or only wants guard tour, can buy exactly that.

The Four Experiences
Who logs in where

One platform, four distinct front doors. Each audience sees only what belongs to them, and three of the four never see the main application at all.

ExperienceWho uses itWhat they do there
Admin application Owners, managers, schedulers, supervisors, finance and sales staff The full product. Sales pipeline, site and guard setup, rostering, patrol configuration and monitoring, incident review, timesheets, invoicing, procurement, integrations, billing and user administration — each person seeing only the pages their role allows.
Guard portal Field guards, on their own phone A mobile-first app: today's shifts, check in and out, walk a patrol route scanning QR checkpoints, file an incident with photo or video evidence, and raise a panic alert.

Why this matters on camera

The strongest demonstration of the product is the hand-off between these four surfaces: a proposal drafted by AI in the admin app, approved by the client in the client portal, a shift created from it, a guard checking in on a phone, and the resulting invoice appearing back in the client portal. That single chain is the whole value proposition.

Separation and isolation

Each experience authenticates separately and carries its own token type. A guard's credentials cannot open the admin application; a client's cannot see another client's invoices; and every query the system runs is scoped to the tenant that owns the data. A second layer, branch scoping, further restricts staff to their own regional office where a company is organised that way.

The platform console — a fifth, internal surface

Separate from all of the above, AegisLead itself operates a Platform Admin console used only by the AegisLead team to see customer accounts, trials, AI spend and credit balances. It is a fully separate identity with its own credentials — a customer administrator, however senior, has no path into it. Covered in section 14.

AegisLead Generation
Win the work

A complete sales system built specifically for security contracts — not a generic CRM. It knows what a guarding proposal looks like, prices it from your own rate cards, and understands that the biggest deals arrive as formal RFPs.

Leads

Capture and qualify prospective customers. The lead page is a guided, step-by-step workflow rather than a blank form — it walks a rep from first contact through qualification to a proposal, showing what to do next at each stage.

  • AI lead scoring — each lead is assessed and ranked so reps work the right ones first.
  • Click-to-dial — call a lead straight from the record; the call is logged, timed, and the outcome recorded.
  • Automatic call recording — calls placed through the app are recorded and attached to the lead; recordings from an external phone dialer can also be uploaded and attached.
  • Notes and activities — a running history on every lead and deal.
  • Import from anywhere — CSV import with field mapping, so a list from any CRM or spreadsheet loads without reformatting, plus direct connectors to eight CRM platforms.
  • Export — leads export to CSV for reporting or hand-off.

Deals and pipeline

Qualified leads convert to deals and move through pipeline stages. Deals can be worked in a list or on a drag-and-drop Kanban board, where moving a card between columns advances the deal stage.

AI Prospect Search

Type a company name and AegisLead produces a complete sales playbook for it: a business summary, the value propositions that apply, concrete sales angles, the personas to approach, and the objections to expect. The result can be imported straight in as a lead.

  • Two modes — a cheap Preview for a quick look, and a Full search for a complete set of results.
  • Saved searches and history — every search is kept; reopening a saved search shows the stored result rather than re-running and re-charging it.
  • Shared cache — if the same company has been researched recently, the cached result is returned instantly and free of charge.
  • Server-side completion — research jobs can take minutes; the server finishes them even if the user closes the tab, and the result is waiting when they return.

Sales Accelerator

An AI coaching workspace that sits over the pipeline. It is the difference between a CRM that records what happened and one that tells a rep what to do next.

Sales Calls

Every call placed from the app, plus any recording uploaded from an external dialer, can be transcribed automatically and analysed. Reps get a searchable transcript, structured discovery analysis and coaching feedback. Recordings can be deleted individually when they are no longer needed.

Proposals

AegisLead drafts the security services proposal itself. The draft is priced from the company's own rate cards, so the numbers in the document are the numbers that will be invoiced.

  • Versioning — every revision is kept and comparable.
  • Client comments — the customer comments directly on the proposal in their portal; the thread stays attached to the document.
  • Approve / reject with a timeline — the client's decision is recorded with a full timeline of who did what and when.
  • Bulk generation — draft proposals across many leads at once.
  • PDF export and email delivery — send it, or hand over a PDF.
  • Placeholder validation — the system refuses to send a proposal that still contains unfilled placeholder blocks.

AegisLead Guard
Prove the work

Everything needed to put guards on the ground and produce defensible evidence that the service was delivered — the part of the business a client will eventually audit.

Sites

The client locations that need guarding. A site carries its address and geographic coordinates, the client it belongs to, and the branch responsible for it. Sites are the anchor for everything downstream: checkpoints, routes, shifts, incidents and reports all hang off a site.

Guards and compliance

  • Guard roster — the workforce, with contact details, branch, skills and portal credentials. Guards can be deactivated rather than deleted, so their history stays intact.
  • Duplicate prevention — the system blocks the same guard being created twice.
  • Guard compliance — licences, certifications and training with expiry dates and uploaded documents. Status is shown plainly so a scheduler can see at a glance who is current and who is not.
  • Availability — guards' availability is recorded and used when rostering.

Patrols — checkpoints, routes and verification

This is the heart of the Guard product, and the part that was rebuilt most recently.

Checkpoints

A checkpoint is a physical point on a site a guard must visit — a fire door, a loading bay, a server room. Each one carries a QR code value and optionally a GPS geofence with a configurable radius (default 50 metres, adjustable from 5 metres to 2 kilometres).

Printable QR sticker sheets

AegisLead generates print-ready QR sticker sheets for a site's checkpoints directly from the app. The sheets are laid out for standard label stock — print them, stick them to the physical checkpoints, and the site is live. No proprietary hardware, no NFC tags to source, no per-device licensing.

Routes

Checkpoints are assembled into an ordered patrol route. A site can have several routes — a perimeter round, an internal round, a night round.

Dual verification

When a guard reaches a checkpoint they scan its QR code with their phone camera. AegisLead records two independent verification results, and stores both:

CheckPossible outcomes recorded
QR verificationMatched · Mismatched (wrong checkpoint scanned) · Not provided · No QR configured
Geofence verificationInside the fence · Outside the fence · Location unavailable · Invalid location · No geofence configured

Keeping them separate matters: a guard whose phone GPS failed indoors but who scanned the correct QR code is a different situation from a guard who scanned the right code from the car park. The record shows which it was.

Patrol runs

A patrol run is one walk of one route by one guard. The system prevents a guard starting two runs at once, tracks live location during the run, and on completion automatically marks any checkpoint that was never scanned as missed — no manual reconciliation, and no way to quietly skip a point.

Patrol Monitor

An aggregate live dashboard for supervisors: which patrols are running right now, across which sites, how far through the route each guard is, and which checkpoints have been missed or failed verification.

Incidents

Guards file incident reports from the field. Each report can carry photo and video evidence attached directly from the phone.

01
Guard files
Report plus photo or video evidence, from the site.
02
Office reviews
A dedicated review queue; approve, amend or reject.
03
Client sees
Only approved reports appear in the client portal.

Nothing reaches the client un-reviewed. That one rule is what makes the client portal safe to give out.

Emergency alerts

A panic button in the guard app. Pressing it raises an immediate alert that surfaces on the admin dispatch screen and sends an email to the responsible contacts. Dispatch acknowledges the alert and then resolves it, and both actions are timestamped — so response time is measurable rather than anecdotal.

Daily service reports

The report the client actually reads. Staff compile a daily service report for a site, AI writes the summary, and the report is published to the client portal and emailed to the client automatically. This is typically the single most visible deliverable a guarding contract produces, and it is generated rather than typed.

AI guard recommendations

When a shift has an unfilled slot, AegisLead suggests which guards to put in it — based on availability, site familiarity, compliance status and branch — and explains in plain language why each one was suggested.

The Guard Mobile Experience
The phone in the field

The guard app is the surface the business depends on most and controls least — it runs on the guard's own phone, in the field, and it has to be simple enough to use one-handed at two in the morning.

A shift, start to finish

1
Sign in. The guard opens the guard portal on their phone and signs in with credentials the office issued them.
2
See today's work. The dashboard shows the shifts assigned to them, with site, times and what still needs doing.
3
Check in. One tap on arrival. This starts the shift, moves it to in progress for the office, and begins the clock that will produce the timesheet.
4
Walk a patrol. The guard picks one of the site's routes and starts a run. Each checkpoint is scanned with the camera; QR and GPS are verified and recorded.
5
Complete the run. Any checkpoint not scanned is automatically marked missed. The run is closed and visible on the Patrol Monitor.
6
Report anything that happened. Incidents are filed from the phone with photos or video attached.
7
Raise an emergency if needed. The panic button is always available and alerts dispatch immediately.
8
Check out. One tap on leaving. This closes the shift and creates the timesheet — the single action that connects the field to the invoice.

Eight taps, and the office knows everything

That sequence is the entire guard-facing product. There is no training course, no separate device and no paperwork — and by the time the guard has checked out, the office already holds the attendance record, the verified patrol evidence, the incident report and the timesheet that will be invoiced.

Guard-side guard rails

  • Check-in gates everything. Patrols cannot be started before the guard has checked in — the sequence is enforced, not suggested.
  • Check-out is validated. A clock-skew guard prevents a phone with a wrong system time producing a nonsense timesheet.
  • No double-starting. A guard cannot have two patrol runs open at the same time.
  • Patrol history. Guards can see their own completed runs.
  • Session handling. The guard app refreshes its own session so a guard is not thrown out mid-shift.

What the office sees in return

Every guard action lands in the admin app immediately: shift status changes from assigned to in progress to completed; attendance events appear against the shift; patrol runs appear on the monitor with their verification results; incidents arrive in the review queue; and a guard who has not checked in by fifteen minutes past their shift start is flagged as a no-show on the dashboard — automatically, and the flag clears itself the moment they arrive.

AegisLead Operations
Schedule it, bill it

Rostering, hours and money. This is where field activity becomes revenue, and it is the product that removes the most manual re-keying from a guarding business.

Shifts and rostering

Shifts are created against a site with a start, an end and a required number of guards. Multi-guard shifts are fully supported: a shift stays open until every required slot is filled, then becomes assigned.

Shift statusMeaning
OpenFewer guards assigned than the shift requires.
AssignedEvery required slot is filled; nobody has arrived yet.
In progressAt least one assigned guard is currently checked in and on duty.
CompletedEvery guard who checked in has checked out — not merely the first one.

A late guard checking in after the others have left correctly flips the shift back to in progress. Status is computed from what actually happened, never stored as a guess.

  • Calendar view — the roster as a calendar, not only a table.
  • AI guard recommendations — suggested guards for every open slot, with reasons.
  • No-show detection — derived live from attendance, with a 15-minute grace period.
  • Shift assignment emails — guards are emailed when they are put on a shift.
  • Branch scoping — schedulers see their own branch's roster.

Timesheets

A timesheet is created automatically when a guard checks out — one per guard per shift, built from the real check-in and check-out times. Office staff review and approve it. Nothing becomes billable without that approval, and nothing has to be typed in from a paper sheet.

Rate cards

The contracted commercial terms per client and site: standard hourly rate, overtime rate and holiday rate. Rate cards are the single source of pricing truth — they price the AI-drafted proposal at the start of the relationship and the invoice at the end of every month, which is what stops the two from disagreeing.

Invoices

01
Generate
Built from approved timesheets at the contracted rates.
02
Issue
Sent to the client and visible in their portal.
03
Settle
Marked paid, cancelled, or disputed and resolved.

Invoices export to PDF for sending and CSV for the accounts package. Every line traces back to an approved timesheet, which traces back to a guard's verified check-in and check-out.

Invoice disputes

Clients raise a dispute against a specific invoice from their own portal, stating what they disagree with. Staff see the dispute in a queue, respond to it, and resolve it. The whole exchange is recorded against the invoice — so a billing argument has a paper trail instead of living in an email thread.

Finance reporting

  • Finance dashboard — totals and status breakdown with charts.
  • Outstanding report — what is owed and how old it is.
  • Payments report — what has been collected.
  • Disputes report — what is contested and where it stands.
  • CSV export — on every report, for the accountant.

Client insurance and compliance

Each client's Certificate of Insurance is tracked, with the document itself stored against the record. The system tracks whether the document is present or missing, shows an advisory banner where it is missing, and exposes the status to the client in their own portal so they can fix it themselves. Uploads are validated by file type on the way in.

The Vendor Portal & Procurement
Subcontracting, run properly

When a contract needs capacity the company does not have — another city, a specialist skill, a short-term surge — AegisLead runs the whole subcontractor procurement as a formal process rather than three phone calls and a forwarded email.

The procurement flow

1
Draft the RFP. Create a Request for Proposal, or have AI generate the whole document from a brief.
2
Analyse the requirements. AI reviews the security requirements in the RFP and flags what the document actually demands of a bidder.
3
Invite vendors. Select vendors from the directory. Each receives an emailed, single-use secure link — no account to create, no password to forget.
4
Collect bids. Vendors open their link, see only their own RFP, and submit a priced proposal with supporting documents. Access closes at the due date.
5
Evaluate with AI. AegisLead produces a structured evaluation report comparing every bid on the same criteria — so the decision is comparable, not impressionistic.
6
Award or reject. Award the contract to the winner and reject the rest. Both outcomes send the vendor an automatic email, and both are written to the audit log.
7
Track performance. The awarded vendor gets performance reviews recorded against them, building a history for the next procurement round.

Related capabilities

  • Generate a proposal from an RFP — when the company is the one bidding, AegisLead turns an incoming RFP into a drafted response.
  • Dual logos — RFP documents carry both the issuing company's and the recipient's branding.
  • Vendor directory — a managed list of subcontractors with their details and performance history.

Security of the vendor link

The vendor portal has no login at all. Access is gated by a cryptographically random, single-purpose invitation token that opens exactly one RFP and expires at that RFP's due date. A vendor cannot see another vendor's bid, the other invitees, or anything else in the system.

Users, Roles & Account Security
Who can do what

Permissions are job-shaped. A non-technical owner picks a role from a short list and gets a sensible result, without reading a permission matrix — but a company that wants fine control can build custom roles from individual permission keys.

The eight built-in roles

RoleWhat they see
Super AdminThe account owner. Everything, including billing, users, branches, integrations, branding and the audit log. The only role with account administration.
ManagerRuns the day-to-day business across sales, field operations and invoicing. Cannot change billing, users, branches or integrations.
SalesThe Generation product only — leads, deals, calls, proposals, RFPs and vendors.
OperationsScheduling and field operations — sites, guards, shifts, patrols, incidents and reports.
SupervisorDeliberately narrow: watches patrols and reviews what is flagged, without touching the roster or any financial data.
FinanceTimesheets, rate cards, invoices, disputes and financial reporting. Does not see the AI sales tools or branches.
GuardThe guard mobile portal only.
ClientThe client portal only.

Beyond these, custom roles can be assembled from a catalogue of roughly 100 fine-grained permission keys across 30 functional areas. A safeguard prevents anyone granting a permission they do not themselves hold, so a role cannot be used to quietly escalate privileges.

Owner protection

The account owner is protected at the system level — they cannot be deleted, demoted or locked out by another administrator, and an administrator cannot remove their own access and strand the account.

Account security

Two-step verification

Email-based two-factor authentication, enabled and disabled by the user themselves from their own security settings with a verification step at each end.

Recovery email

A separate, verified recovery address so a user who loses access to their primary inbox is not locked out permanently.

Security alert emails

The account is notified by email when sensitive security settings change, so an unauthorised change is visible immediately.

One active session

Each user is limited to a single active login — the newest wins. A shared password becomes obvious instantly, and the displaced session is told clearly why it ended rather than failing silently.

Audit trail

Security-sensitive actions are written to a tenant-scoped audit log: logins, role changes, forced logouts, denied access to restricted fields, and contract awards and rejections. It is readable from the admin app by anyone with audit permission.

Field-level permissions

A further layer can hide or lock specific sensitive fields — a guard's pay rate, a client's billing notes — from roles that should not see them, even on a record they otherwise have access to. Enforcement runs on every request.

Branches

Companies operating in several regions model those as branches. Staff, sites, guards and shifts belong to a branch, and non-owner staff are scoped to their own — so a regional manager sees their region, and only their region.

Billing, Credits & Self-Service
Getting paid

AegisLead sells itself. A customer can sign up, choose a package, pay by card and be provisioned without anyone at AegisLead touching the account.

Subscriptions

1
Choose a package and band. The plan page shows exactly what is included and what it costs at the customer's current guard count.
2
Pay by card. Checkout runs through Stripe; AegisLead never touches card details.
3
Get provisioned automatically. The subscription confirmation unlocks exactly the modules that were paid for — resolved from the purchased price itself, never from editable metadata.
4
Manage it themselves. Customers update their card, view invoices and manage the subscription through a self-service billing portal.

Entitlements

What a customer paid for is enforced everywhere — the web app, the public API and every background process. The sidebar, the command palette and the API all agree, so a module that was not bought is not visible and not reachable. Attempting to use a feature outside the plan returns a clear, distinct response that lets the interface offer an upgrade rather than show an error.

How the credit system works

Reserve, then settle

A job's true cost is not known until it finishes. The system holds the maximum up front, then charges the actual amount and returns the rest. A failed job is refunded in full; a cached result costs nothing.

An append-only ledger

Every credit movement is a permanent ledger row. Corrections are new rows, never edits, and the ledger total must always equal the balance. A customer can see exactly where every credit went.

Low-balance warnings

Billing contacts are emailed before credits run out, rather than discovering it when a search fails.

Automatic top-up

Customers can opt in to have a pack purchased automatically when the balance runs low — and control the thresholds themselves. Entirely optional.

Abandoned holds released

A hold left behind by a closed tab or a restart is swept automatically after an hour, so credits can never be frozen permanently.

Refund and chargeback handling

Credits are clawed back automatically on a refund or chargeback, so a customer cannot buy, spend, refund and keep the usage. Partial refunds are escalated to a human rather than guessed at.

What the customer sees

  • Your Plan — current package, guard band, what is included, and what an upgrade would cost.
  • Search Credits — balance, the full ledger of what was spent on what, pack purchase and auto top-up settings.
  • Billing — card management, invoices and subscription controls.

Architecture, Security & Reliability
How it is built

The stack

LayerTechnology

Security measures in place

  • Tenant isolation — every query is scoped to the owning tenant, enforced throughout the application.
  • Token-based authentication with separate token types for admin, client and guard, and tracked sessions.
  • Role-based access control on every administrative route, with self-escalation prevented.
  • Hashed API keys — the plaintext is shown once and never stored.
  • Signed webhooks — every outbound payload is cryptographically signed.
  • Encrypted third-party credentials — connector tokens are encrypted at rest.
  • Outbound request protection — customer-supplied webhook URLs are validated to prevent them being used to reach internal systems.
  • Upload validation — uploaded documents are checked by file type on the way in.
  • Single active session per user, with a clear message when a session is superseded.
  • Email two-step verification, verified recovery addresses, and security alert emails.
  • Audit logging of security-sensitive actions.

Reliability

  • Encrypted nightly database backups held off-site in separate object storage.
  • Automated uptime monitoring with health probes on every deployment.
  • Continuous integration — the full test suite runs on every change before it can ship.
  • Separate staging environment configured identically to production.
  • Shared caching and rate limiting across application instances, so the platform scales horizontally without duplicated work or duplicated charges.

Performance

A dedicated performance pass removed the heaviest costs in the application: the dashboard no longer loads every lead, deal and proposal in order to render a handful of summary lines; an unused animation library was removed entirely; and the support chat widget is held back until the page is idle so it cannot delay the first view.
